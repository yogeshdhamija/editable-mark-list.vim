*editable-mark-list.txt*  The mark list as an editable buffer

Author: Yogesh Dhamija <yogeshdhamija@outlook.com>

                                                                   *:MarkList*
:MarkList		Show the marks in the current window as a plain, editable
			buffer. Edits take effect immediately -- there is
			nothing to save -- and the list updates itself as marks
			change elsewhere.

The list shows the local marks of the buffer it replaced, then the global
ones, then the special ones, with hints above it: >

  see :h :MarkList | Updates Live!
  <CR> to jump | delete line to :delmarks | u to undo
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Local (a-z)
   a     12  let g:loaded = 1
   t     40  function! s:Tidy(lines) abort

  Global (A-Z)
   V  ~/.vimrc:37  nnoremap `  <Cmd>call AccessMarks()<CR>

  Special
   "     12  position when last exiting buffer
   .     44  position of last change
<
Local and special marks show their line number, global and numbered ones
their file too. Then the text of that line, when its buffer is loaded, or
what a special mark is. The hints and section titles are not part of the
buffer. They need Neovim or Vim 9.0.0451+.

' and ` (the position before the latest jump) are left out: they belong to a
window, not a buffer.

==============================================================================
USAGE

<CR>		Jump to the mark under the cursor, as |`| would from the
		buffer the list replaced. CTRL-O comes back to the list.
		For a local mark, CTRL-^ afterwards goes where it would
		have before the list.

`{mark}  '{mark}
		Jump to {mark}, as shown in the list: a local mark is
		the buffer's the list replaced, not the list's own.
		`` and '' go back to where you were in that buffer.

Deleting lines	Any way of deleting lines -- `dd`, `5dd`, `Vd`, `:g/foo/d` --
		deletes those marks. ^ and . can't be: their lines come
		back, with a message. They can't be set again, so
		deleting them couldn't be undone.

Adding lines	Putting a deleted line back (`p` after `dd`), or typing a
		mark on a line of its own, puts back a mark deleted from
		a list, where it was. Any other new line is swept away.

                                                     *editable-mark-list-undo*
u  CTRL-R	Undo or redo your last change to the list; takes a count.
		Marks come back exactly where they were, column too. A
		mark set again since stays where it is now. History is
		shared by all lists and survives leaving the list. Vim's
		other undo commands (`U`, `g-`, |:undo|, ...) do nothing
		here.

Only the mark at the start of a line matters. Mangle the rest and the line is
just redrawn.

==============================================================================
` WITH THE LIST ON SHOW                              *editableMarkList#Pick()*

editableMarkList#Pick()
	|`|, with the list shown in the current window while Vim waits for
	the mark. Map ` to it: >
		nnoremap ` <Cmd>call editableMarkList#Pick()<CR>
<	Then, after `:
	    {mark}	the built-in `{mark} runs: the jumplist, the
			alternate file, errors and macros are exactly as
			without the mapping
	    <Esc>	the list stays, in Normal mode: `<Esc>gg, `<Esc>yy
	    CTRL-C	back where you were, nothing changed
	    other keys	the list stays, and the key runs in it: `<CR>
			jumps to the first mark, `/foo searches the list,
			and a click lands in it

	Showing the list and taking it away again leaves no trace: no
	autocommands fire, and the jumplist, alternate file, view and |'quote|
	mark are as they were.

	Nothing is drawn when the mark is already typed ahead (`a typed
	quickly, or from a mapping or |:normal|) or a register is executing.

	Macros: recording `a stores `a, and replaying it jumps to a. A
	macro that recorded `<Esc> stays in the list when replayed, as it did
	when recorded.

	Visual and Operator-pending mode (v`a, d`a) are untouched, since the
	mapping is for Normal mode only. So are windows that can't show the
	list: the command-line window, 'winfixbuf' and 'diff' windows, and
	popups. There ` works as before, and <Esc> cancels.

==============================================================================
OPTIONS

                                                  *g:editable_mark_list_hints*
g:editable_mark_list_hints		(default: 1)
	Set to 0 to hide the hints. The section titles stay.

==============================================================================
HIGHLIGHTS

	markListHint		the hints			Comment
	markListHintKey		keys and commands in the hints	Special
	markListHeader		section titles			Title

	markListLnum		line numbers			Number

In Vim (not Neovim), a hint line has the one color, markListHint.
